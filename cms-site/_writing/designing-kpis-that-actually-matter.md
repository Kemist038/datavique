Most dashboards carry twenty numbers and drive two decisions. The rest are there because they were easy to calculate.

## The four-question test

1. **Who acts on it?** If no one owns the number, it’s a statistic, not a KPI.
2. **What would they do differently?** Name the decision before the measure.
3. **How fast does it move?** A KPI reviewed weekly must be able to change weekly.
4. **Can it be gamed?** Pair every target with a counter-measure.

> “Name the decision before you name the measure.”

## An example from e-commerce

“Average delivery days” looks useful but hides the thing customers punish: missing the promised date. “Share of orders delivered after estimate” is owned by logistics, moves weekly and maps directly to review scores.
