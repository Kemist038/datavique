Ask a plant manager why the monthly report can’t be trusted and the answer is usually “the data is bad.” But data is only a record of what people did in the system — or didn’t do. To find the real fault line, walk the chain.

*ERP → Data quality → Processes → People → Reporting → Decisions*

## 1. The ERP is a data system first

Every sales order, purchase receipt and stock entry is a row with a timestamp. The ERP isn’t only software for running operations; it is the largest source of operational data the company owns.

## 2. Quality is set at the point of entry

Missing BOM revisions, late stock postings and free-text rejection reasons all surface downstream as “bad data.” No pipeline can repair a transaction that was never recorded correctly.

> “No dashboard can be more accurate than the process that feeds it.”

## 3. Reporting inherits every gap

By the time data reaches Power BI, each upstream shortcut has compounded. The fix is rarely a better chart — it’s a model that makes gaps visible and a process that stops creating them.

## What to do instead

Start with the three transactions that drive most decisions — usually stock movement, production and sales — and measure how often they’re posted late or incomplete. That number is a better diagnosis than any data-quality tool.
